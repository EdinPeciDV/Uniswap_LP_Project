A year of LP returns, $100k per strategy

ETH fell 37% over the period. Unhedged positions mostly follow ETH down; the hedge strips out the price bet and leaves fees against impermanent loss.

An LP is short volatility

Hedged, the position earns fees (theta) and pays impermanent loss (gamma) of about σ²/8 a year, the same quantity as loss-versus-rebalancing (LVR).

Fees didn't cover the volatility

Narrow ranges scale both sides

Going from full range to ±5% lifts fee APR from 3% to 59%, but gamma rises faster, and every re-centre locks in the loss.

Fees vs volatility cost over time

Full-range LP, rolling 30 days, annualised. Fees covered the volatility cost only while the blue line sat above the orange one. After January 2026 that rarely happened.

Where the hedged P&L came from

Tighter ranges earn far more in fees, and lose even more to impermanent loss after hedging. Funding on the short perp is a steady positive.

Step one: the model matches mainnet exactly

Before trusting a backtest, I checked the math against what the contracts actually did, using the most recent ~9,000 Ethereum blocks.

Method and limitations
  • 8,761 hourly archive reads of slot0, liquidity and feeGrowthGlobal0/1X128 for the USDC/WETH 0.05% pool, via Multicall3.
  • An in-range position with liquidity L earns exactly L × ΔfeeGrowthGlobal, diluted by its own share of active liquidity.
  • Rebalanced ranges re-centre when the hourly price leaves the range, paying the 0.05% pool fee on the swap plus $5 gas.
  • Hedges short delta on the Hyperliquid ETH perp, re-hedged daily, with a 0.045% taker fee and actual hourly funding.
  • Limits: hourly granularity, no JIT/MEV modelling, hedge fills at the pool price, one pool over one bearish year.
